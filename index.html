User's Guide

How Fishermen's Buddy fits together

Getting Started

What the app does and the five tabs

Fishermen's Buddy is your personal fishing log and forecast. You log the fish you catch; the app quietly records the conditions behind every catch — tide, weather, moon, pressure, water temperature, and location — and uses your own history to tell you when and where the bite is likely to be on.

The more you log, the smarter it gets. Early on it leans on fish biology; over time it leans on you.

The five tabs

  • Home — your forecast for right now: the best window today and a 7-day outlook for the fish you target.
  • Log — record a catch. This is the heart of the app and what powers everything else.
  • Map — every catch you've made, pinned on a map.
  • Analytics — the conditions you've actually caught each fish in: your personal patterns.
  • More — your Trips, Species, Personal Records, Settings, and this guide.
New here? Add the fish you target (More → Species), then log your next catch. After a handful of catches your forecasts and patterns start to mean something.

Logging a Catch

The form, auto-captured conditions, tide & offline

Open the Log tab, pick the species, and tap Save Catch. That's the only required step — everything else is optional or automatic.

What you can add

  • Length and weight — optional, but they're what unlock Personal Records and the "new personal best" celebration.
  • A photo — snap one or pick from your library.
  • Lure / bait and notes — handy for spotting what's working.

Conditions are captured for you

You never type in the weather. When you log a catch, the app pulls the live tide, water temperature, wind, pressure (and whether it's rising or falling), cloud cover, and moon phase for your exact spot and time. That's the data your forecasts and Analytics are built from.

Tide phase

Tide phase is pulled automatically for your location. The app uses the nearest NOAA station when one is within range — NOAA covers US waters and a number of international ports; for anywhere else it falls back to a global tidal model (WorldTides). The source is shown in teal on the Log screen, and in the header of the tide chart pop-out on Home. If neither source is reachable (typically a signal issue), the tide phase row shows "No tide data" — it will update automatically once connectivity is restored. On remote flats and back-country, the real tide can run earlier or later than the reported station. If it's off, tap the tide phase and pick what the water is actually doing where you're standing. It defaults to your last pick, so correcting a new spot is one tap — and it keeps your records and patterns accurate.

No signal? No problem.

Your catch saves right away even with no service, and syncs automatically once you're back in range. We then backfill the weather, tide, and moon for that exact spot and time — so you never lose a catch or have to enter conditions by hand.

Caught something out of its range?

If you try to log a fish in a spot we didn't think it lives, we'll ask before saving. Tap "Add this area" and we save the catch and start forecasting that fish here, scored like any other. Tap "Exit" and nothing is saved — perfect if you picked the wrong species by accident, so you can choose again. Your catches are the final word on where a fish lives: catch one somewhere we didn't expect and we'll always offer to add it.

Beat your previous best length or weight for a species and you'll get a confetti shower celebrating the new personal best.

Forecast & Best Window

The Home screen, scores and the WHY breakdown

The Home screen answers one question: how good is the fishing, and when's the best time to go?

The Best Window card

The big number is the best score your selected fish reaches today, on a 0–100 scale. Alongside it is the window of time that score lands in.

  • 90+ Excellent · 70+ Good · 50+ Fair · under 50 Poor.
  • Below the window you'll see today's live conditions — tide, water temp, moon, wind, pressure, and sky (e.g. partly cloudy · 60% sunshine).
  • Tap the Tide row to pop out a full tide chart — a smooth curve of the day's rise and fall, with the high and low turns marked, a live NOW line at the current tide, and the upcoming highs and lows listed below. The header of the chart tells you the data source: NOAA CO-OPS when a NOAA station is within range, or WorldTides (a global tidal model) for locations without one. If the Tide row shows "No tide data", neither source could reach your location — usually a signal issue; it will resolve once connectivity is restored.
  • Tap the Moon row to pop out the moon phase — the current phase drawn to scale, how lit it is, a plain-language note on why it matters for the bite, and the dates of the next full and new moon. (Phase and dates are accurate to about a day; precise moonrise/set times come later.)
  • Tap the Sunshine row for today's sunrise and sunset, day length, a live countdown to the next one, and a day/night arc showing where the sun is now. Sunshine shows the clear-sky share during daylight and reads 0% after dark.
  • Tap the card to see WHY — a breakdown of which conditions are helping or hurting the score right now, plus a plain-language note if season is limiting it.

Hour by hour, day by day

  • The bars show the score across the day. Tap any bar to see that hour's reasons.
  • The 7-day outlook scores each upcoming day from real forecast data. Tap a day to drill into it.
  • Some fish — sight fisheries like bonefish, permit, and tarpon — only feed in daylight. For these, the hourly bars and Best Window cover daytime hours only; nighttime hours are intentionally hidden. You can override this in More → Species → Edit Parameters if that fish bites at night in your area.

Switching fish

Swipe the cards at the bottom to switch between the species you target, or tap + to add a new one. The add list has a search bar — find a fish by name or category instead of scrolling. Each fish is scored on its own preferences, so the best window for snook can be very different from the best window for trout.

How Scores Work

Biology to start, your catches over time

Every score is a weighted blend of how well the current conditions match what a fish likes — water temperature, tide phase, time of day, moon, wind, pressure and its trend, and cloud cover.

Season and range can cap a score

Conditions aren't the whole story — the fish has to actually be around. When you tap WHY, the Season note at the top tells you in plain language if it's a slow month, shoulder season, or peak — and whether season is capping the score even when tide and weather look perfect. If a species is out of its range entirely — you're outside the areas it lives — the score is 0, because conditions don't matter when the fish isn't there. Travelling away from home? Fish from back home may read 0 until you return. And if we've got a fish's range wrong, logging a catch there lets you set us straight (see Logging a Catch).

It starts with biology

Before you've logged anything, each species starts from sensible defaults drawn from fish biology and regional seasonality. That's why you get useful forecasts on day one.

Then it learns from you

Every catch you log nudges that fish's preferences toward the conditions you actually catch it in. The blend is gradual on purpose — a few catches only tilt it slightly, so one lucky outing doesn't hijack your forecast. As your history grows, your real data takes over from the biology estimate.

Daylight-only fish

Some fish can only be effectively caught in daylight — sight fisheries like bonefish, permit, and tarpon rely on seeing their prey in clear, lit water and aren't really fishable after dark. For these, the hourly bars and Best Window on Home are limited to daylight hours only. If a fish shows no night hours, that's why. You can override the setting in More → Species → Edit Parameters if you catch that species at night in your area.

Tuning it yourself (optional)

If you think you know a fish better than the defaults, you can edit its preferences directly — see Species & Groupings. Two things drive each score: how much each factor matters (its weighting) and the ideal conditions for that fish.

You don't need to tune anything. Logging catches is the main way the app gets accurate — the manual settings are just there if you want them.

Analytics & Your Patterns

Reading your fingerprints and the learning meter

The Analytics tab shows the conditions you've actually caught each fish in — your personal patterns, built only from the catches you've logged. Pick a fish at the top to see its patterns.

Reading the charts

  • Taller bars are conditions you've caught that fish in more often.
  • The faint grey bars behind them are the starting estimate from fish biology — so you can see where your experience agrees with or differs from the textbook.

Personalization vs. confidence — two different things

Near the bottom is the PERSONALIZATION bar. It shows one thing: overall, how much your own catches outweigh our biology starting estimate for this fish. It climbs every time you log a catch. But — and this matters — a high personalization bar does not automatically mean every chart is trustworthy. Those are two separate questions, and we treat them separately.

Why some patterns firm up faster than others

It comes down to how many possibilities a condition has. Water temperature has a single sweet spot, so a few dozen catches pin it down well. Tide and moon are different — each has eight separate phases, so your catches get spread thin across all of them. Ten catches split across eight tide phases is barely one per phase: nowhere near enough to trust a "best tide" yet, even though those same ten catches are plenty to trust a temperature range.

So we judge each condition on its own merits, not on your total catch count. As a rough guide: simple conditions like water temp, wind, and pressure become reliable around 25–30 catches; time of day around 50; tide and moon need closer to 90, because they have the most phases to fill in.

The confidence badge

Open any chart and you'll see a small colored badge on its summary, telling you how far that one condition has come:

  • Early (amber) — just a hint. We show what you have, but it mostly reflects when you happened to fish. Treat it as a curiosity, not advice.
  • Emerging (blue) — a real lean is showing through. Worth paying attention to, still firming up.
  • Reliable (green) — enough catches that the pattern is genuinely yours. Trust it.

We're deliberately careful with strong claims. The app won't crown a "top tide" or "best moon phase" until one phase clearly stands out from the rest — so the headline won't flip every time you log a single new catch. And every read shows the count behind it ("4 of 11 catches"), so you can always see how much it's built on rather than just a percentage.

Don't wait for everything to turn green before the app is useful. Your forecasts lean on the biology estimate underneath from day one — the badges are only about how much to trust your PERSONAL pattern for that one condition.

Expanded charts

Tap any chart to open a detailed view. Each factor gets a visualization built around how that condition actually works:

  • Tide Phase — a sine wave showing one full tidal cycle. The 7 spaces between the grey bars each represent one tide phase. Each dark red dot is a catch placed randomly in its phase's space. A grey count above the arc shows the total for each space. High Slack — the brief pause at the top — is the center space at the wave's peak.
  • Temperature — a histogram of your catch temperatures, with your sweet spot highlighted, your average marked, and the biology seed shown for comparison.
  • Pressure Trend — a rainbow histogram showing how many catches you've had at each 0.10 inHg pressure band. The color runs from blue (low pressure, stormy) on the left through green (stable) to red (high pressure, clear) on the right. BEST RANGE marks the pressure window where your catches cluster most. The Y-axis rescales as you log more, so the tallest bar always fills the chart.
  • Time of day, moon, wind, and cloud cover — bar charts showing which conditions your catches cluster in, plus a plain-language summary.
To archive or delete a fish, go to More → Species — Analytics is just for viewing your patterns.

Catch Map

Pins, species & date filters, satellite

The Map tab plots every catch you've logged, with a pin for each one.

  • Pins are colored by species, so you can see at a glance where each fish comes from.
  • Use the filters to show only certain species, or narrow to a date range.
  • Tap a pin to open that catch's full details.
  • Switch to satellite view to read structure, drop-offs, and shoreline.
Over a season the map turns into a heat-map of your productive water — a great way to plan where to start.

Trips

Your fishing diary, backfilling, and merging

A trip groups together all the catches from one outing — it's your fishing diary.

  • A trip is created automatically when you log your first catch of an outing. You don't have to start or stop anything.
  • Open a trip (More → Trips) to see every catch from that session and the conditions behind them.
  • It's the quickest way to look back at how a day went and what was working.

Editing a trip

Tap the pencil icon on any trip to set its start time, end time, name, and location. Tap a date or time field to pick it — no typing.

By default a trip runs from your first catch to your last catch. Setting a start or end only ever WIDENS that window — if you try to set a start after your first catch or an end before your last, you'll see an error naming the catch that's in the way. That's what lets you record an overnight session (say 9 PM to 3 AM across two days) or any outing where you were on the water longer than the fish were biting.

Your times stick. Log a catch later than the end you set and the trip stretches to include it; a catch before the end won't pull it back. Delete that later catch and the trip returns to the end you set. Tap "Mark still open" to clear the end and leave the trip in progress.

An active (in-progress) trip shows the time of your latest catch as a running end in the header, so you can see the session's span growing even before you close it. Trips that cross midnight show a date range — e.g. Jun 4–5 — so you always know which days the outing spans.

Setting real start and end times keeps duration-based stats (like time on the water) honest.

Forgot to log a catch from a past trip?

Open that trip from More → Trips and tap ADD. The catch is added directly to the trip, and the conditions are pulled from the trip's own records — not today's live data. If you need to fine-tune the exact time, open the catch and tap the pencil icon. If the time you enter falls outside the original trip window, the trip automatically expands to include it.

On the Log screen, tap the location row to drop a custom pin on the map — useful if this catch happened at a different spot within the same outing.

Moving a catch to a different day

Open any catch and tap the pencil icon to edit it. If you change the date to a different day — say you logged a fish as today's when it was actually yesterday's — the app looks at how far apart that date is from the rest of the catches in the same trip.

  • Small difference (a few hours, or an overnight session that runs past midnight): the trip's window just stretches automatically to include the new time. No prompt, nothing to decide.
  • Clearly a different day (more than 16 hours from the other catches): the app asks you what to do.

When the app asks, you have three choices:

  • Merge into existing trip — if a trip already exists on the target date, you can fold this catch straight into it. Say you had a morning bonefish trip on the 5th and you're moving a late catch over there: merge puts all the fish from both outings into one tidy trip.
  • New separate trip — creates a brand-new trip just for this catch on the new date. Good when you want two clean, separate outings.
  • Keep in this trip — leaves the catch in its current trip and lets the window stretch to cover both days. The right call for genuine overnight sessions that run from one calendar day into the next.
If moving the catch leaves the original trip completely empty, the app deletes it automatically — no cleanup needed on your end.

Forgot to log an entire trip?

To backfill a whole outing you forgot to log:

  • Go to the Log tab and enter all the catches. They'll land on today's date to start with.
  • Open each catch (More → Trips → tap the trip → tap a catch → pencil) and change its date and time to when you actually caught it.
  • The first catch you move to the real day starts a fresh trip on that date; move the rest to the same day and the app offers to merge them into it — so the whole outing ends up together.
Once every catch is on the right day, open the trip and set its true start and end times if you want the window to match how long you were actually out.

Species & Groupings

Tracking fish, custom species, fine-tuning

Your species list (More → Species) is the set of fish you target. These show up on the Home carousel and in Analytics.

  • Use the search bar at the top to find a fish by name or nickname — handy once your list gets long. Common regional names work: "redfish" finds Red Drum, "beeliner" finds Vermilion Snapper, "specs" finds Spotted Seatrout.
  • Add a species by name — tap Add and start typing. As you type, any matching species appear instantly in the dialog, including regional nicknames and aliases ("redfish" finds Red Drum, "ahi" finds Yellowfin Tuna, "beeliner" finds Vermilion Snapper). A partial word like "snapper" or "grouper" surfaces every species in that family. If your fish is already there, tap EXIT. If nothing matches, tap ADD SPECIES, then pick the group it fits (Flats, Offshore Reef, Freshwater Predators, and so on).
  • For a new fish, we estimate its preferred conditions, seasonality, and behavior from that group as a starting point — then you fine-tune everything (and set the areas it lives) anytime by opening it from the list.
  • Every built-in species has a photo in the list. Tidy your list by archiving or removing fish you don't target (see below).

Archiving or removing a fish

Tap the ••• beside a fish you added to Archive it or Delete it. Archive hides it from your list but keeps every catch — it returns automatically when you log that species again, and until then it sits in an "Archived" section at the bottom of the list where you can restore it. Delete permanently removes it and all its catches. Built-in species can't be removed, so they have no ••• control.

Species Groupings

Groups are families of fish that behave alike — Flats, Offshore Pelagic, Freshwater Predators, and so on. A new custom species borrows its starting behavior from its group. Edit a group's defaults (More → Species Groupings) and those become the starting point for any custom species you add to it. Standard species keep their own tuned settings.

Fine-tuning a single fish

Open a species to edit its parameters: how much each factor matters (weighting), its preferred water temp, wind, and pressure, the tide / time / moon phases it bites best in, whether it needs clear skies ("Best sky" — mainly for sight-fishing flats fish), and whether it's day-only (night hours are hidden on Home — typical for sight fisheries like bonefish, permit, and tarpon; override it here if the fish bites at night in your area). You can reset to the defaults anytime.

Season in your area

The same screen shows how we rate a fish 0–5 for every month, broken out by area — so you can check our seasonality against what you actually see on the water. Tap an area to open it, tap "Adjust" to grade each month yourself, or "Add an area" if you catch it somewhere we didn't list. It's read-only until you tap Adjust, so you can't change anything by accident. Reset a single area — or the whole fish — back to our defaults whenever you like.

As always, a fish's own logged catches gradually take over from any default or manual setting — so you can't really break anything here.

Personal Records

Biggest fish, best days, streaks, PR confetti

Personal Records (More → Personal Records) collects your fishing milestones, all pulled automatically from the catches you've logged.

  • Your longest and heaviest of each species.
  • Your most productive trips and your best single day.
  • Your longest streak of consecutive days with a catch.

These update every time you log a catch — there's nothing to enter separately.

For a clean leaderboard of every species you've landed — ranked by how many you've caught, each with its longest and heaviest — open More → Species Caught. (That's different from More → Species, which is the full list of fish you can track, caught or not.)

When a catch beats your previous best length or weight for a species, the Log screen celebrates it with a confetti shower. Add a length or weight when you log to make those moments count.

Good-Day Alerts

Heads-up when a great day is coming

Good-day alerts give you a heads-up when the forecast first reveals an upcoming day that one of your tracked fish is expected to bite well.

  • You set the bar with a score threshold, and the alert fires whenever a tracked species is forecast to peak at or above whatever you've set it to. It starts at 85 out of the box, but if you've lowered it (say to 80), then days that peak at 80–84 will start alerting too — so the number in the alert can be below 85.
  • You get the heads-up as soon as that day comes into view, and only once per day — no spam.
  • Turn alerts on or off, and adjust the threshold, in Settings.

Each upcoming day is judged on its own and pinged the first time it crosses your threshold — the alert flags new good days as they appear, it doesn't always point to your single highest-scoring day. So if a day you can see in the 7-day forecast scored well but you never got an alert for it, it most likely sent that alert on an earlier day, before it scrolled closer. A day is only ever alerted once, so it also won't ping again if its score climbs higher later.

Want to compare every upcoming day side by side? The 7-day forecast on the Home screen always shows them all — alerts are just a nudge so you don't have to keep checking.
If alerts don't appear, make sure notifications are allowed for the app in your phone's system settings.

Settings & Account

Units, theme, notifications, your account

Open Settings from the gear icon on the Home screen (top right).

  • Units — switch between Imperial (inches, pounds, °F) and Metric (cm, kg, °C). Every screen updates instantly.
  • Appearance — Dark, Light, or Auto (follows your phone).
  • Notifications — turn good-day alerts on/off and set the score threshold.
  • Account — sign out, or change your password via email.

Glossary

Tide phases, pressure trend, moon, and more

Tide phases

The app splits the tide into eight phases, from slack water through the flood and ebb:

  • Low slack — the brief pause at the bottom of the tide.
  • Early / Mid / Late flood — the rising tide, building toward high.
  • High slack — the pause at the top of the tide.
  • Early / Mid / Late ebb — the falling tide, draining toward low.

On the Tide Phase chart in Analytics, the eight phases are laid out as a sine wave. The 7 spaces between the grey bars each represent one phase, reading left to right: E.Flood, M.Flood, L.Flood, High Slack (center, at the wave peak), E.Ebb, M.Ebb, and L.Ebb. A grey count above the arc shows how many catches fell in each space. Low Slack (dead low) is the starting and ending point at both ends of the wave.

Pressure & pressure trend

Barometric pressure is measured in inches of mercury (inHg) — typical sea-level readings run from about 29.5 (stormy) to 30.5 (clear and calm). Many fish feed hard on a falling barometer ahead of a front, then slow once pressure settles high, so the trend (Rising / Steady / Falling) often matters as much as the number itself. The Analytics Pressure Trend chart shows both: a rainbow histogram of the raw pressure bands your catches cluster in, alongside a separate trend breakdown.

Moon phase & illumination

How lit the moon is, from new (dark) to full. It shifts feeding times and — around new and full moons — drives bigger tide swings.

Water temperature

Sea-surface temperature at your spot. It's one of the strongest drivers of where fish hold and how actively they feed.

Sky & sunshine

Cloud cover from live weather. On the Best Window card it's shown as a sky description plus sunshine percent (e.g. partly cloudy · 60% sunshine) — 60% sunshine means 40% cloud cover. For sight-fishing species, clear skies can matter more; see Best sky in Edit Parameters.
